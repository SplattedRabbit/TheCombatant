5701: looks    for    opportunities    to    find    and

5704: Characteristics:  A  ranger  can  use  a  variety  
5705: of  weapons  and  is  quite  capable  in  combat.  
5706: His   skills   allow   him   to   survive   in   the   
5707: wilderness, to find his prey, and to avoid 
5708: detection.     He     also     has     special     
5709: knowledge   about   certain   types   of   
5710: creatures, which makes it easier for him to find and defeat such foes. 
5711: Finally,  an  experienced  ranger  has  such  a  tie  to  nature  that  he  can  
5712: actually  draw  upon  natural  power  to  cast  divine  spells,  much  as  a  
5713: druid does. 
5714: Alignment: Rangers can be of any alignment. Most are good, and 
5715: such rangers usually function as protectors of the wild areas. In this 
5716: role,  a  ranger  seeks  out  and  destroys  or  
5717: drives  off  evil  creatures  that  threaten  
5718: the wilderness. Good rangers also protect 
5719: those  who  travel  through  the  wilderness,  
5720: serving sometimes as guides and sometimes 
5721: as  unseen  guardians.  Most  rangers  are  also  
5722: chaotic,  preferring  to  follow  the  ebb  and  flow  
5723: of  nature  or  of  their  own  hearts  instead  of  rigid  
5724: rules.  Evil  rangers,  though  rare,  are  much  to  be  
5725: feared.    They    revel    in    nature’s    thoughtless    
5726: cruelty and seek to emulate her most fearsome 
5727: predators. They gain divine spells just as good 
5728: rangers do, for nature herself is indifferent to 
5729: good and evil. 
5730: Religion:   Though   a   ranger   gains   his   
5731: divine  spells  from  the  power  of  nature,  he  
5732: like  anyone  else  may  worship  a  chosen  
5733: deity.   Ehlonna   (goddess   of   the   wood-
5734: lands)  and  Obad-Hai  (god  of  nature)  are  
5735: the   most   common   deities   revered   by,   
5736: though some prefer more martial deities. 
5737: Background:   Some   rangers   gained   
5738: their  training  as  part  of  special  military  
5739: teams, but most learned their skills from

5742: particular  master  may  count  themselves  as  
5743: cohorts, or they may be rivals for the status

5745: to their master’s fame.

5814: GAME RULE INFORMATION 
5815: Rangers have the following game statistics. 
5816: Abilities:  Dexterity  is  important  for  a  ranger  both  because  he  
5817: tends to wear light armor and because several ranger skills are based 
5818: on that ability. Strength is important because rangers frequently get 
5819: involved  in  combat.  Several  ranger  skills  are  based  on  Wisdom,  and  
5820: a Wisdom score of 14 or higher is required to get access to the most 
5821: powerful  ranger  spells.  A  Wisdom  score  of  11  or  higher  is  required  
5822: to  cast  any  ranger  spells  at  all.  One  of  the  ranger’s  trademark  skills,  
5823: his ability to track foes, is based on Wisdom. 
5824: Alignment: Any. 
5825: Hit Die: d8.

5827: Class Skills 
5828: The ranger’s class skills (and the key ability for each skill) are Climb 
5829: (Str),  Concentration  (Con),  Craft  (Int),  Handle  Animal  (Cha),  Heal

5831: Knowledge   (geography)   (Int),   Knowledge   (nature)   (Int),   Listen

5833: (Int),  Spot  (Wis),  Survival  (Wis),  Swim  (Str),  and  Use  Rope  (Dex).

5838: Class Features 
5839: All of the following are class features of the ranger. 
5840: Weapon and Armor Proficiency: A ranger is proficient with all 
5841: simple   and   martial   weapons,   and   with   light   armor   and   shields   
5842: (except tower shields). 
5843: Favored  Enemy  (Ex):  At  1st  level,  a  ranger  may  select  a  type  of  
5844: creature  from  among  those  given  on  Table  3–14:  Ranger  Favored  
5845: Enemies.  Due  to  his  extensive  study  on  his  chosen  type  of  foe  and  
5846: training in the proper techniques for combating such creatures, the 
5847: ranger  gains  a  +2  bonus  on  Bluff,  Listen,  Sense  Motive,  Spot,  and  
5848: Survival  checks  when  using  these  skills  against  creatures  of  this  
5849: type.  Likewise,  he  gets  a  +2  bonus  on  weapon  damage  rolls  against  
5850: such creatures. 
5851: At  5th  level  and  every  five  levels  thereafter  (10th,  15th,  and  20th  
5852: level), the ranger may select an additional favored enemy from those 
5853: given  on  the  table.  In  addition,  at  each  such  interval,  the  bonus  
5854: against any one favored enemy (including the one just selected, if so 
5855: desired)  increases  by  2.  For  example,  a  5th-level  ranger  has  two  
5856: favored  enemies;  against  one  he  gains  a  +4  bonus  on  Bluff,  Listen,  
5857: Sense  Motive,  Spot,  and  Survival  checks  and  weapon  damage  rolls,  
5858: and  against  the  other  he  has  a  +2  bonus.  At  10th  level,  he  has  three  
5859: favored enemies, and he gains an additional +2 bonus, which he can 
5860: allocate  to  the  bonus  against  any  one  of  his  three  favored  enemies.  
5861: Thus, his bonuses could be either +4, +4, +2 or +6, +2, +2. 
5862: If the ranger chooses humanoids or outsiders as a favored enemy, 
5863: he must also choose an associated subtype, as indicated on the table. 
5864: If  a  specific  creature  falls  into  more  than  one  category  of  favored  
5865: enemy   (for   instance,   devils   are   both   evil   outsiders   and   lawful   
5866: outsiders),   the   ranger’s   bonuses   do   not   stack;   he   simply   uses   
5867: whichever  bonus  is  higher.  See  the  Monster  Manual  for  more  infor-
5868: mation on types of creatures.

5944: Animal  Companion  (Ex): At 4th level, a ranger gains an animal 
5945: companion  selected  from  the  following  list:  badger,  camel,  dire  rat,  
5946: dog, riding dog, eagle, hawk, horse (light or heavy), owl, pony, snake 
5947: (Small or Medium viper), or wolf. If the DM’s campaign takes place 
5948: wholly  or  partly  in  an  aquatic  environment,  the  DM  may  add  the  
5949: following   creatures   to   the   ranger’s   list   of   options:   crocodile,   
5950: porpoise,   Medium   shark,   and   squid.   This   animal   is   a   loyal   
5951: companion   that   accompanies   the   ranger   on   his   adventures   as   
5952: appropriate  for  its  kind.  (For  instance,  an  aquatic  creature  can’t  
5953: adventure  with  a  ranger  on  land  and  shouldn’t  be  selected  by  a  
5954: nonaquatic  character  without  extenuating  circumstances).  In  most  
5955: cases,  the  animal  companion  functions  as  a  mount,  sentry,  scout,  or  
5956: hunting animal, rather than as a protector. 
5957: This ability functions like the druid ability of the same name (see 
5958: page 35), except that the ranger’s effective druid level is one-half his 
5959: ranger  level.  For  example,  the  animal  companion  of  a  4th-level  
5960: ranger   would   be   the   equivalent   of   a   2nd-level   druid’s   animal

5966: would  be  reduced  by  3,  to  1st  level.  Like  a  druid,  a  ranger  cannot  
5967: select  an  alternative  animal  if  the  choice  would  reduce  his  effective  
5968: druid level below 1st. 
5969: Spells:  Beginning  at  4th  level,  a  ranger  gains  the  ability  to  cast  a  
5970: small  number  of  divine  spells  (the  same  type  of  spells  available  to  
5971: the  cleric,  druid,  and  paladin),  which  are  drawn  from  the  ranger  
5972: spell  list  (page  191).  A  ranger  must  choose  and  prepare  his  spells  in  
5973: advance (see below).

5975: equal  to  at  least  10  +  the  spell  level  (Wis  11  for  1st-level  spells,  Wis  
5976: 12 for 2nd-level spells, and so forth). The Difficulty Class for a saving 
5977: throw  against  a  ranger’s  spell  is  10  +  the  spell  level  +  the  ranger’s  
5978: Wisdom modifier. 
5979: Like other spellcasters, a ranger can cast only a certain number of 
5980: spells  of  each  spell  level  per  day.  His  base  daily  spell  allotment  is  
5981: given  on  Table  3–13:  The  Ranger.  In  addition,  he  receives  bonus  
5982: spells  per  day  if  he  has  a  high  Wisdom  score  (see  Table  1–1:  Ability  
5983: Modifiers and Bonus Spells, page 8). When Table 3–13 indicates that 
5984: the  ranger  gets  0  spells  per  day  of  a  given  spell  level  (for  instance,  
5985: 1st-level spells for a 4th-level ranger), he gains only the bonus spells 
5986: he  would  be  entitled  to  based  on  his  Wisdom  score  for  that  spell  
5987: level.  The  ranger  does  not  have  access  to  any  domain  spells  or  
5988: granted powers, as a cleric does. 
5989: A ranger prepares and casts spells the way a cleric does, though he 
5990: cannot  lose  a  prepared  spell  to  cast  a  cure spell in its place. A ranger 
5991: may prepare and cast any spell on the ranger spell list, provided that 
5992: he  can  cast  spells  of  that  level,  but  he  must  choose  which  spells  to  
5993: prepare during his daily meditation. 
5994: Through  3rd  level,  a  ranger  has  no  caster  level.  At  4th  level  and  
5995: higher, his caster level is one-half his ranger level.

6083: ROGUE 
6084: Rogues  share  little  in  common  with  each  other.  Some  are  stealthy  
6085: thieves.  Others  are  silver-tongued  tricksters.  Still  others  are  scouts,  
6086: infiltrators, spies, diplomats, or thugs. What they share is versatility, 
6087: adaptability,  and  resourcefulness.  In  general,  rogues  are  skilled  at  
6088: getting  what  others  don’t  want  them  to  get:  entrance  into  a  locked  
6089: treasure  vault,  safe  passage  past  a  deadly  trap,  secret  battle  plans,  a  
6090: guard’s trust, or some random person’s pocket money. 
6091: Adventures: Rogues adventure for the same reason they do most 
6092: things:  to  get  what  they  can  get.  Some  are  after  loot;  others  want  
6093: experience.  Some  crave  fame;  others  seek  infamy.  Quite  a  few  also  
6094: enjoy  a  challenge.  Figuring  out  how  to  thwart  a  trap  or  avoid  an  
6095: alarm is great fun for many rogues. 
6096: Characteristics:  Rogues  are  highly  skilled,  and  they  can  con-
6097: centrate on developing any of several categories of skills. While not 
6098: equal  to  members  of  many  other  classes  in  combat,  a  rogue  knows  
6099: how to hit where it hurts, and she can dish out a lot of damage with a 
6100: sneak attack. 
6101: Rogues  have  a  sixth  sense  when  it  comes  to  avoiding  danger.  
6102: Experienced   rogues   develop   mystical   powers   and   skills   as   they   
6103: master  the  arts  of  stealth,  evasion,  and  sneak  attacks.  In  addition,  
6104: while not capable of casting spells on their own, rogues can “fake it” 
6105: well  enough  to  cast  spells  from  scrolls,  activate  wands,  and  use  just  
6106: about any other magic item. 
6107: Alignment:   Rogues   follow   opportunity,   not   ideals.   They   are   
6108: more  likely  to  be  chaotic  than  lawful,  they  are  a  diverse  bunch,  so  
6109: they may be of any alignment. 
6110: Religion:  Although  they  are  not  renowned  for  their  piety,  most  
6111: rogues   revere   Olidammara   (god   of   thieves).   Evil   rogues   might   
6112: secretly  worship  Nerull  (god  of  death),  or  Erythnul  (god  of  slaugh-
6113: ter).  Since  rogues  are  so  diverse,  however,  many  of  them  worship  
6114: other deities, or none at all. 
6115: Background:   Some   rogues   are   officially   inducted   into   an   
6116: organized  fellowship  of  rogues  or  “guild  of  thieves.”  Some  are  self-
6117: taught; others learned their skills from independent mentors. Often, 
6118: an experienced rogue needs an assistant for scams, second-story jobs, 
6119: or  just  for  watching  her  back.  She  recruits  a  likely  youngster,  who  
6120: then learns the skills of the trade on the job. Eventually, the trainee 
6121: is ready to move on, perhaps because the mentor has run afoul of the 
6122: law,  or  perhaps  because  the  trainee  has  double-crossed  her  mentor  
6123: and needs some “space.” 
6124: Rogues do not see each other as fellows unless they happen to be 
6125: members  of  the  same  guild  or  students  of  the  same  mentor.  In  fact,  
6126: rogues trust other rogues less than they trust anyone else. They’re no 
6127: fools. 
6128: Races:  Adaptable  and  often  unprincipled,  humans  take  to  the  
6129: rogue’s   life   with   ease.   Halflings,   elves,   and   half-elves   also   find   
6130: themselves  well  suited  to  the  demands  of  the  career.  Dwarf  and  
6131: gnome  rogues,  while  less  common,  are  renowned  as  experts  with  
6132: locks and traps. Half-orc rogues tend toward thuggery. 
6133: Rogues are common among brutal humanoids, especially goblins 
6134: and bugbears. Rogues who learn their arts in savage lands, however, 
6135: generally  don’t  have  much  experience  with  complex  mechanisms  
6136: such as traps and locks. 
6137: Other  Classes:  Rogues  love  and  hate  working  with  members  of  
6138: other classes. They excel when protected by warriors and supported 
6139: by  spellcasters.  There  are  plenty  of  times,  however,  that  they  wish  
6140: everyone  else  was  as  quiet,  guileful,  and  patient  as  they.  Rogues  are  
6141: particularly  wary  of  paladins,  so  they  endeavor  to  prove  themselves  
6142: useful when contact with paladins is unavoidable. 
6143: Role:  The  rogue’s  role  in  a  group  can  vary  dramatically  based  on  
6144: her  skill  selection—from  charismatic  con  artist  to  cunning  burglar  
6145: to  agile  combatant—but  most  rogues  share  certain  aspects.  They  
6146: aren’t   capable   of   prolonged   melee   combat,   so   they   focus   on   
6147: opportunistic  sneak  attacks  or  ranged  attacks.  The  rogue’s  stealth  
6148: and  her  trapfinding  ability  make  her  one  of  the  best  scouts  in  the  
6149: game.
